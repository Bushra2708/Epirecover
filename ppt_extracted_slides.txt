=== ppt/slides/slide2.xml ===
Abstract EpiRecover Ancient Indian inscriptions carry rulers, dynasties, donors and events, but weathering, erosion and missing stone destroy characters, and OCR can read only what survives. EpiRecover is a damage-aware pipeline that segments damaged regions, reads visible text with explicit gap markers, generates candidate fillings from linguistic and retrieved evidence, ranks them with a learned fusion score, calibrates confidence, and abstains when evidence is insufficient. It is evaluated by ablation on synthetic damage of increasing severity, with a leakage-safe split by inscription, and ends in expert verification. Keywords: computational epigraphy, damaged text reconstruction, multimodal fusion, retrieval, calibration, abstention 2

=== ppt/slides/slide3.xml ===
Introduction Why inscriptions matter Inscriptions record rulers, dynasties, donors, places, administration, languages and events. Why they are hard to read Physical damage (erosion, cracks, missing stone) and imaging damage (shadows, perspective, low resolution). Where current AI stops Enhancement and OCR improve visible text; they cannot recover physically absent characters. Research question: can multimodal evidence reconstruct damaged inscriptions while identifying insufficient-evidence cases? 3

Objectives 1. Damage-aware preprocessing Build preprocessing and segmentation that labels visible, damaged and missing regions. 2. Gap-aware OCR Read only what survives and mark gaps instead of guessing. 3. Synthetic damage benchmark Create damage levels D0–D6 with ground truth and splits by inscription. 4. Candidate generation Propose readings from language-model and retrieval evidence. 5. Fusion and calibration Learn evidence weights; calibrate confidence (ECE, Brier). 6. Abstention and evaluation Abstain when evidence is weak; ablate evidence sources across damage severity. 9
